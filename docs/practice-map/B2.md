# B2 Practice Map

Status: **first full map drafted**

This is a level library map, not a required lesson sequence.

`Core` means **Great place to start**, not prerequisite.

## Design boundary

B2 Practice should feel spontaneous, strategically demanding, and meaningfully less predictable than B1 while remaining grounded in plausible familiar/semi-familiar life situations.

Typical B2 mission shape:

- a clear goal with **competing constraints, trade-offs, or disagreement**
- roughly 6–10+ meaningful learner moves depending on branches
- learner must explain priorities and consequences, not just state a preference
- alternatives may each solve one problem while creating another
- polite disagreement and pushback are expected
- learner initiative materially affects the route
- AI wording/turn length can be more natural and less scripted
- several valid endings may exist

B2 should not depend mainly on subtle social implication, high-stakes relationship management, sophisticated register shifts, or intentionally ambiguous motives. Those become central at C1/C2.

Detailed language grounding is selected when a mission is promoted to a full contract. The B2 map is informed by reviewed B2 abilities in `addvaluewithai-hub/english-course`, including:

# Mission library

## Everyday Life — 4 missions

### B2-EV-01 — Negotiate a solution to a shared practical problem ★ Core

**Goal:** resolve a familiar shared-life problem where both sides have legitimate needs and no first option fully satisfies both.

**Communication problem:** `competing needs + negotiation + compromise`

**Possible contexts:** shared flat noise/cleaning schedule, borrowed item damaged, shared transport plan, responsibility for a practical mistake.

**Likely shape:** establish problem -> each side explains priority -> learner challenges/clarifies assumption -> propose solution -> counterproposal -> compare consequences -> agree compromise and responsibilities.

**B1 -> B2 progression:** B1 explains a practical problem and chooses a solution. B2 must actively negotiate between competing needs.

**Listening Preview:** optional/recommended for an early B2 Core mission.

---

### B2-EV-02 — Defend a practical choice when challenged

**Goal:** explain and sustain a decision about a familiar practical issue when another person questions it, while responding directly to their objections.

**Communication problem:** `justify position + respond to counterargument`

**Possible topics:** changing routine, choosing transport, buying/renting option, organising shared time, using a service differently.

**Likely shape:** state decision -> partner challenges one reason -> learner explains evidence/consequence -> partner raises alternative -> learner acknowledges/contrasts -> refine or maintain position -> close.

**B2 boundary:** familiar stakes and explicit objections; subtle identity/status issues belong at C1.

**Listening Preview:** usually unnecessary.

---

### B2-EV-03 — Resolve conflicting versions of what happened

**Goal:** compare two different accounts of a familiar incident, identify what is agreed/uncertain, and reach a practical next step without assuming one side is automatically right.

**Communication problem:** `conflicting information + clarification + resolution`

**Possible contexts:** missed handoff, shared booking, damaged borrowed item, delivery instruction, schedule change.

**Likely shape:** hear conflicting account -> state own account -> ask precise questions -> identify common facts -> distinguish uncertainty -> propose next step based on what is known -> confirm.

**B1 -> B2 progression:** B1 clears up a misunderstanding. B2 manages disagreement about facts and uncertainty.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

---

### B2-EV-04 — Coordinate a change with two practical constraints

**Goal:** revise an existing arrangement while balancing two explicit constraints such as time + cost, availability + location, or convenience + responsibility.

**Communication problem:** `re-plan + trade-off + agreement`

**Likely shape:** change announced -> identify constraints -> generate options -> compare consequences -> reject one option with reason -> adapt proposal -> confirm final plan.

**B2 boundary:** constraints are explicit and practical. Hidden interpersonal sensitivity belongs at C1.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

## People & Social — 3 missions

### B2-PS-01 — Disagree politely and keep the conversation constructive ★ Core

**Goal:** disagree with a friend/acquaintance on a familiar topic, explain why, acknowledge part of their view, and continue productively rather than ending in yes/no opposition.

**Communication problem:** `polite disagreement + argument + relationship-preserving continuation`

**Possible topics:** lifestyle choices, entertainment, travel preferences, study/work habits, everyday technology, organising social activities.

**Likely shape:** partner states view -> learner disagrees/qualifies -> gives reasons -> partner counters -> learner acknowledges useful point -> responds/adjusts -> find limited agreement or agree to differ.

**B1 -> B2 progression:** B1 expresses and supports an opinion. B2 handles active disagreement over several turns.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

---

### B2-PS-02 — Compare competing recommendations and persuade a friend

**Goal:** recommend one option over another when both have clear advantages, explain trade-offs, respond to objections, and try to reach a shared decision without forcing agreement.

**Communication problem:** `compare trade-offs + persuade + respond`

**Possible contexts:** trip, event, course, neighbourhood, restaurant, activity, purchase with social relevance.

**Likely shape:** compare options -> state recommendation -> partner raises concern -> explain trade-off -> answer challenge -> revise proposal or defend it -> decision.

**B2 boundary:** persuasion remains everyday and cooperative; high-stakes influence is out of scope.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

---

### B2-PS-03 — Help two people find common ground in a minor disagreement

**Goal:** clarify what each person actually wants in a low-stakes disagreement and suggest a practical area of agreement.

**Communication problem:** `light facilitation + reframing + common ground`

**Possible contexts:** choosing activity, shared plan, division of simple responsibilities, group preference.

**Likely shape:** person A position -> person B position -> learner asks neutral clarification -> restates priorities -> identifies overlap -> proposes compromise -> parties respond -> summarise agreement.

**B2 boundary:** this is **minor practical facilitation**, not complex mediation involving power, sensitive identity, or layered motives; those belong at C1/C2.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

## Food & Shopping — 4 missions

### B2-FS-01 — Negotiate a service solution after a failed order ★ Core

**Goal:** resolve a service failure when the obvious replacement is not available and several imperfect solutions exist.

**Communication problem:** `complaint + competing solutions + negotiation`

**Likely shape:** explain failure -> confirm facts -> requested fix unavailable -> partner offers partial solution -> learner explains why insufficient -> propose/counterpropose -> agree replacement/refund/credit/alternative within truth.

**B1 -> B2 progression:** B1 resolves a wrong order with a straightforward correction. B2 negotiates when no straightforward correction exists.

**Listening Preview:** optional/recommended.

---

### B2-FS-02 — Challenge a service/refund decision and seek a reasonable alternative

**Goal:** question an initial service decision, explain why it does not address the situation, and negotiate a reasonable alternative without becoming adversarial.

**Communication problem:** `policy constraint + pushback + alternative solution`

**Likely shape:** request action -> partner cites policy/limit -> learner asks rationale -> explains relevant circumstances -> proposes alternative -> partner modifies/offers option -> learner evaluates -> agreement or clear unresolved close.

**Authoring note:** scenario truth must define policy boundaries so Gemini cannot invent arbitrary exceptions.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

---

### B2-FS-03 — Choose a product under competing requirements

**Goal:** choose between several products when budget, quality/features, timing, or use-case requirements conflict.

**Communication problem:** `requirements + trade-off analysis + decision`

**Likely shape:** explain use case/priorities -> partner proposes option -> learner identifies drawback -> compare second option -> revise priority -> choose and justify.

**B1 -> B2 progression:** B1 explains needs and evaluates a recommendation. B2 must explicitly trade one desirable feature against another.

**Listening Preview:** usually unnecessary.

---

### B2-FS-04 — Resolve a disputed bill or charge

**Goal:** challenge a charge that appears inconsistent with the agreed purchase/service, compare records/details, and negotiate a clear resolution.

**Communication problem:** `dispute facts + explain evidence + negotiate correction`

**Likely shape:** identify charge -> explain discrepancy -> partner provides explanation -> learner questions/compares detail -> identify source of difference -> agree correction/payment split/verification next step.

**B2 boundary:** use fictional low-stakes amounts and clear authored evidence. Do not turn this into financial advice or fraud investigation.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

## Travel & Transport — 4 missions

### B2-TT-01 — Rebook after a cancellation under time/cost constraints ★ Core

**Goal:** recover from a cancelled journey when available alternatives differ in arrival time, cost, route, or convenience.

**Communication problem:** `disruption + competing constraints + negotiation`

**Likely shape:** establish cancellation -> state key priorities -> hear options -> ask consequences -> reject unsuitable option -> propose/ask another -> compare trade-offs -> choose/rebook.

**B1 -> B2 progression:** B1 chooses among a small set after disruption. B2 must manage meaningful trade-offs and actively shape the solution.

**Listening Preview:** optional/recommended.

---

### B2-TT-02 — Negotiate a hotel solution after a repeated problem ★ Core

**Goal:** resolve a repeated or serious-enough hotel/service problem when the first offered fix does not fully solve the learner's needs.

**Communication problem:** `recurring complaint + inadequate first solution + negotiation`

**Likely shape:** explain history/problem -> partner offers first fix -> learner explains remaining issue -> state priority -> ask/propose alternative -> partner counters -> agree workable solution.

**B1 -> B2 progression:** B1 reports the problem and accepts/asks one alternative. B2 challenges an insufficient solution and negotiates among constraints.

**Listening Preview:** optional/recommended.

---

### B2-TT-03 — Resolve a disputed ticket, fee, or transport charge

**Goal:** explain why a transport charge/ticket seems incorrect or unfair under the authored facts, understand the other side's position, and seek a practical resolution.

**Communication problem:** `dispute + factual argument + negotiated next step`

**Likely shape:** state issue -> official explains rule -> learner presents relevant facts -> official challenges/clarifies -> learner responds -> discuss available action/appeal/payment option -> conclude.

**Authoring note:** keep it fictional and focused on language practice, not real legal advice.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

---

### B2-TT-04 — Coordinate a group trip with competing preferences

**Goal:** build a travel plan for several people when time, cost, comfort, or activity preferences conflict.

**Communication problem:** `multi-person preferences + trade-offs + collaborative decision`

**Likely shape:** establish preferences -> identify conflict -> compare options -> learner proposes compromise -> partner pushes one priority -> adjust/defend proposal -> agree plan.

**B2 boundary:** explicit practical preferences; complex stakeholder politics belong at C1/C2.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

## Work & Study — 3 missions

### B2-WS-01 — Push back on an unrealistic deadline ★ Core

**Goal:** explain why a proposed deadline is unrealistic, show the consequences, challenge the plan professionally, and negotiate a workable alternative.

**Communication problem:** `professional pushback + evidence + negotiation`

**Likely shape:** deadline assigned -> learner explains constraints/progress -> partner insists/asks why -> learner describes consequences -> proposes options -> partner counteroffers -> agree scope/time trade-off.

**B1 -> B2 progression:** B1 explains a deadline problem and agrees a next step. B2 actively negotiates scope/time/quality trade-offs.

**Listening Preview:** optional/recommended.

---

### B2-WS-02 — Discuss project priorities and agree trade-offs

**Goal:** decide what should be prioritised when several familiar tasks matter but resources/time are limited.

**Communication problem:** `prioritise + justify + negotiate trade-offs`

**Likely shape:** review tasks -> learner prioritises with reasons -> partner challenges priority -> discuss consequence -> reprioritise/defend -> agree order/ownership.

**B2 boundary:** keep domain expertise light; communication strategy, not specialised knowledge, should drive difficulty.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

---

### B2-WS-03 — Disagree with a proposed approach in a meeting

**Goal:** challenge a familiar work/study proposal, explain concerns, respond to counterarguments, and help reach a revised plan or explicit decision.

**Communication problem:** `formal disagreement + argument + revision`

**Likely shape:** proposal presented -> learner questions assumption -> explains concern -> partner defends -> learner acknowledges/answers -> proposes adjustment -> agree or record unresolved point.

**B2 -> C1 boundary:** B2 manages clear disagreement competently; C1 will add subtler status, tone, ambiguity, and relationship risk.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

## Home & Services — 3 missions

### B2-HS-01 — Negotiate a solution after a failed repair/service visit ★ Core

**Goal:** resolve a situation where a paid/routine service did not fix the problem and the provider's first response creates an unacceptable cost/time outcome.

**Communication problem:** `service failure + responsibility + negotiation`

**Likely shape:** explain previous service and current issue -> provider proposes paid/new visit -> learner challenges responsibility -> compare evidence/terms -> propose solution -> provider counters -> agree action.

**B1 -> B2 progression:** B1 explains a recurring problem and schedules a fix. B2 negotiates who should do/pay/when after a failed attempt.

**Listening Preview:** optional/recommended.

---

### B2-HS-02 — Resolve a billing or subscription dispute

**Goal:** challenge a service bill/subscription change, understand the provider's explanation, and negotiate a correction or acceptable next step.

**Communication problem:** `billing discrepancy + policy explanation + negotiated resolution`

**Likely shape:** identify issue -> provider explains charge/term -> learner points out expectation/evidence -> ask precise question -> provider offers option -> learner evaluates/pushes back -> agreement/cancellation/escalation path.

**Authoring note:** fictional low-stakes service; no real financial advice.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

---

### B2-HS-03 — Choose a service plan under time/cost/quality trade-offs

**Goal:** choose or modify a familiar service plan when no option is best on every dimension.

**Communication problem:** `requirements + trade-offs + condition negotiation`

**Possible contexts:** internet package, repair option, delivery service, class/gym plan, home service schedule.

**Likely shape:** explain requirements -> hear plans -> compare limits -> ask whether one condition can change -> provider responds -> choose or negotiate bounded modification.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

## Plans & Leisure — 3 missions

### B2-PL-01 — Build a group plan around competing preferences ★ Core

**Goal:** help a small group reach a plan when people care about different things such as budget, timing, location, or activity type.

**Communication problem:** `competing preferences + compromise + group decision`

**Likely shape:** collect priorities -> identify conflict -> propose option -> one person objects -> compare consequences -> adapt compromise -> confirm plan and responsibilities.

**B1 -> B2 progression:** B1 re-plans after one complication. B2 balances several people's competing priorities.

**Listening Preview:** optional/recommended.

---

### B2-PL-02 — Re-design an event plan under budget and time constraints

**Goal:** revise a leisure/event plan when the original idea is too expensive, too late, or otherwise impractical, while preserving the main goal.

**Communication problem:** `constraints + alternatives + trade-off`

**Likely shape:** discover constraint -> clarify what must be preserved -> propose alternatives -> compare cost/time/experience -> reject weak option -> combine/choose solution -> confirm.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

---

### B2-PL-03 — Resolve disagreement about responsibilities for a shared event

**Goal:** deal with disagreement about who should do/pay/organise what, explain fairness concerns, and negotiate a clear division of responsibilities.

**Communication problem:** `responsibility dispute + justification + compromise`

**Likely shape:** disagreement stated -> each side explains view -> learner identifies concrete responsibilities -> challenge imbalance -> propose split -> counterproposal -> agree responsibilities.

**B2 boundary:** practical low-stakes disagreement. Relationship-sensitive resentment/subtext belongs at C1.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

## What B2 deliberately leaves for C1+

B2 should not make its main difficulty depend on:

- reading subtle implication or hidden interpersonal meaning
- highly sensitive feedback or criticism
- deliberate register switching across audiences
- tact under significant power imbalance
- managing ambiguity where facts/intentions remain incomplete
- reframing emotionally loaded positions with high precision
- extended mediation of layered stakeholder conflict
- deliberately indirect communication where pragmatic inference is central

B2 can negotiate clear competing positions. C1 adds **nuance, ambiguity, register, and relationship-sensitive pragmatics**.

# Cross-level progression checks from B1

```text
B1 solve a wrong order
-> B2 negotiate when no straightforward correction is available

B1 explain a deadline problem
-> B2 push back and negotiate scope/time/quality trade-offs

B1 recover from travel disruption
-> B2 shape the rebooking around competing time/cost constraints

B1 accept/reject one offered alternative
-> B2 generate/counterpropose alternatives and justify trade-offs

B1 complaint with requested action
-> B2 challenge an inadequate/policy-limited response

B1 re-plan after one complication
-> B2 build compromise across several competing preferences

B1 express an opinion with reasons
-> B2 sustain the view through active counterargument
```

If a B2 mission has only one obvious solution and never requires the learner to prioritise, push back, or manage disagreement/trade-offs, it is probably B1 rather than B2.
