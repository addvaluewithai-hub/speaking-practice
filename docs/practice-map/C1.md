# C1 Practice Map

Status: **first full map drafted**

This is a level library map, not a required lesson sequence.

`Core` means **Great place to start**, not prerequisite.

## Design boundary

C1 Practice should foreground **nuance, precision, register, ambiguity, tact, and relationship-sensitive choices**.

Typical C1 mission shape:

- a complex but plausible professional/social goal
- incomplete information or more than one reasonable interpretation
- the learner must choose not only **what** to say, but **how** to say it
- explicit and implicit concerns may coexist
- tactful challenge, reframing, and audience adaptation matter
- longer turns are natural, but length alone is not the difficulty
- several discourse paths may be valid
- the learner may need to summarise, reframe, or steer the interaction toward a conclusion

C1 should not become an abstract intellectual puzzle. The situation should remain recognisably human and useful.

## Core / Great place to start

One Core mission per World keeps C1 broad without pretending there is a sequence:

1. `C1-EV-01` — Handle ambiguous responsibility after a practical failure
2. `C1-PS-01` — Give sensitive feedback without damaging the relationship
3. `C1-FS-01` — Handle a serious service complaint with tact
4. `C1-TT-01` — Resolve a complex accommodation problem diplomatically
5. `C1-WS-01` — Give difficult professional feedback
6. `C1-HS-01` — Escalate a persistent service issue professionally
7. `C1-PL-01` — Navigate a sensitive group disagreement about plans

Listening Preview is **selective** at C1. Use it when modelling pragmatic shape/register adds genuine value; do not routinely reduce uncertainty before every mission.

# Mission library

## Everyday Life — 3 missions

### C1-EV-01 — Handle ambiguous responsibility after a practical failure ★ Core

**Goal:** resolve a practical failure when responsibility is not obvious, accounts are incomplete, and accusing the other person too directly could damage cooperation.

**Communication problem:** `ambiguity + responsibility + tactful resolution`

**Possible contexts:** shared property damaged, important handoff missed, booking/action not completed, shared obligation fell through.

**Likely shape:** establish impact -> compare incomplete accounts -> learner separates facts from assumptions -> asks precise neutral questions -> acknowledges own possible role where appropriate -> frames responsibility carefully -> proposes fair next step -> confirm.

**B2 -> C1 progression:** B2 negotiates between clear positions. C1 must negotiate while the facts/responsibility themselves remain partly uncertain.

**Listening Preview:** optional/recommended.

---

### C1-EV-02 — De-escalate a tense practical conversation

**Goal:** keep a tense but ordinary disagreement productive, acknowledge frustration without accepting inaccurate claims, and steer the interaction toward a workable conclusion.

**Communication problem:** `emotion + reframing + de-escalation`

**Likely shape:** partner expresses frustration/blame -> learner acknowledges concern -> corrects/qualifies facts carefully -> reframes shared goal -> proposes path -> partner resists or softens -> learner steers toward conclusion.

**C1 boundary:** emotional/pragmatic control is central, but stakes remain everyday rather than crisis/safety situations.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

---

### C1-EV-03 — Explain the same complex decision to two different audiences

**Goal:** explain one nuanced practical decision first to an informal peer/friend and then to a more formal service/professional contact, adapting register while preserving the same core meaning.

**Communication problem:** `audience adaptation + register shift + precision`

**Likely shape:** explain decision informally -> respond to personal reaction -> context shifts -> restate relevant facts formally -> answer challenge/clarification -> confirm action.

**C1 progression:** difficulty comes from adapting tone/detail/register, not from obscure topic knowledge.

**Listening Preview:** usually unnecessary.

## People & Social — 3 missions

### C1-PS-01 — Give sensitive feedback without damaging the relationship ★ Core

**Goal:** tell a friend/peer that a recurring behaviour caused a problem, while being specific, fair, and relationship-aware.

**Communication problem:** `sensitive feedback + evidence + relationship preservation`

**Likely shape:** open carefully -> describe behaviour/impact without exaggeration -> other person reacts defensively/uncertainly -> learner clarifies intention -> acknowledges their perspective -> asks/proposes change -> agree next step.

**B2 -> C1 progression:** B2 handles clear disagreement. C1 must phrase criticism so the relationship and face needs are managed deliberately.

**Listening Preview:** recommended if used as a pragmatic exemplar, not a script.

---

### C1-PS-02 — Respond to an indirect concern and uncover what the person really means

**Goal:** recognise that a friend/acquaintance is expressing concern indirectly, ask tactful questions, and respond to the underlying issue rather than only the literal words.

**Communication problem:** `implication + clarification + supportive response`

**Likely shape:** partner hints at concern -> learner notices ambiguity -> asks non-intrusive question -> partner reveals more -> learner checks interpretation -> responds/advises appropriately -> close.

**C1 boundary:** authored signals must be interpretable; do not turn this into mind-reading.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

---

### C1-PS-03 — Mediate a relationship-sensitive misunderstanding

**Goal:** help two people with different interpretations of a social incident understand each other and agree how to move forward.

**Communication problem:** `mediation + perspective reframing + tact`

**Likely shape:** hear person A -> hear person B -> learner separates intention/impact -> reframes both positions neutrally -> checks shared ground -> invites solution -> summarises agreement.

**B2 -> C1 progression:** B2 can facilitate a minor practical disagreement. C1 handles more emotional ambiguity and relationship consequences.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

## Food & Shopping — 3 missions

### C1-FS-01 — Handle a serious service complaint with tact ★ Core

**Goal:** explain a significant service failure clearly, distinguish the core issue from secondary frustrations, and negotiate an appropriate resolution without losing professionalism.

**Communication problem:** `complex complaint + prioritisation + tactful escalation`

**Likely shape:** establish history/impact -> staff explains constraints -> learner identifies what matters most -> challenges weak response precisely -> acknowledges legitimate constraint -> proposes proportionate solution -> escalate/resolve while maintaining tone.

**B2 -> C1 progression:** B2 negotiates clear service trade-offs. C1 manages tone, proportionality, and relationship with the service provider while escalating appropriately.

**Listening Preview:** optional/recommended.

---

### C1-FS-02 — Negotiate an exception when policy wording is ambiguous

**Goal:** discuss a service/purchase case where policy can reasonably be interpreted more than one way, clarify the rule, and argue for an exception or fair interpretation with precision.

**Communication problem:** `ambiguous policy + precise argument + pragmatic negotiation`

**Likely shape:** staff cites policy -> learner asks exact meaning/scope -> identifies relevant ambiguity -> explains circumstances -> staff resists/qualifies -> learner reframes request -> reach exception/alternative/clear unresolved close.

**Authoring note:** scenario truth must define the ambiguity explicitly; Gemini must not invent legal rights.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

---

### C1-FS-03 — Resolve an expectation mismatch without accusing the other side

**Goal:** explain that a product/service technically matches the description but does not reasonably meet the expectation created in context, and negotiate a fair outcome.

**Communication problem:** `expectation vs literal compliance + nuanced complaint`

**Likely shape:** describe mismatch -> provider points to literal terms -> learner acknowledges factual point -> explains broader expectation/impact -> asks targeted question -> proposes proportionate remedy -> resolve.

**C1 boundary:** this tests nuanced framing, not consumer-law expertise.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

## Travel & Transport — 3 missions

### C1-TT-01 — Resolve a complex accommodation problem diplomatically ★ Core

**Goal:** manage an accommodation problem involving several linked issues and an imperfect service response, prioritising what must be fixed now versus what can be compromised.

**Communication problem:** `multi-issue complaint + prioritisation + tact`

**Possible scenario truth:** room type wrong + late arrival + only partial alternative available; noise + accessibility/request mismatch; booking transfer confusion.

**Likely shape:** explain history succinctly -> identify primary need -> staff explains constraints -> learner distinguishes essential vs flexible requirements -> proposes/assesses alternatives -> tactfully escalate if needed -> confirm solution.

**B2 -> C1 progression:** B2 negotiates explicit hotel trade-offs. C1 adds prioritisation, precision, and diplomatic escalation across a more complex history.

**Listening Preview:** optional/recommended.

---

### C1-TT-02 — Explain an unusual travel requirement to different staff

**Goal:** communicate a non-routine but non-medical travel requirement accurately across two staff roles, adapting detail/register and correcting assumptions without sounding confrontational.

**Communication problem:** `complex requirement + audience adaptation + repair`

**Likely shape:** explain need to first staff -> receive partial/mistaken interpretation -> clarify -> transfer/escalate -> restate concisely to second staff -> answer questions -> confirm arrangement.

**Authoring note:** use fictional operational needs; avoid medical/safety-critical dependence.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

---

### C1-TT-03 — Resolve ambiguous responsibility between two travel providers

**Goal:** deal with a disruption where two providers each point to the other, clarify responsibilities, avoid circular blame, and secure a practical next step.

**Communication problem:** `multi-party ambiguity + precise clarification + escalation`

**Likely shape:** provider A denies scope -> learner summarises known facts -> provider B position introduced -> learner distinguishes responsibility from immediate need -> asks who can authorise next action -> reframes request -> obtain action/escalation path.

**C1 boundary:** not legal adjudication; success is communicative clarity and practical progress.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

## Work & Study — 3 missions

### C1-WS-01 — Give difficult professional feedback ★ Core

**Goal:** give a colleague/classmate specific critical feedback on work or behaviour while maintaining respect, inviting response, and agreeing improvement.

**Communication problem:** `critical feedback + tact + collaborative improvement`

**Likely shape:** frame purpose -> give specific observation -> explain impact/standard -> colleague responds/defends -> learner acknowledges valid point -> clarify concern -> agree next action.

**B2 -> C1 progression:** B2 disagrees with a proposal. C1 critiques another person's performance/behaviour where face and relationship matter.

**Listening Preview:** optional/recommended.

---

### C1-WS-02 — Challenge a manager/teacher decision tactfully

**Goal:** question a decision made by someone with more authority, explain concerns precisely, and propose an alternative without sounding dismissive or insubordinate.

**Communication problem:** `power difference + tactful challenge + alternative`

**Likely shape:** authority states decision -> learner asks rationale -> acknowledges objective -> raises concern/consequence -> authority pushes back -> learner reframes/proposes modification -> agree/reject with professional close.

**C1 boundary:** power/relationship dynamics are explicit; no coercion or high-stakes employment/legal advice.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

---

### C1-WS-03 — Facilitate a meeting with conflicting priorities and unclear goals

**Goal:** help a familiar team clarify what decision is actually needed, surface conflicting priorities, and steer the discussion toward a usable conclusion.

**Communication problem:** `facilitation + ambiguity + synthesis`

**Likely shape:** discussion starts unfocused -> learner summarises positions -> identifies missing decision criterion -> asks targeted questions -> reframes disagreement -> proposes decision frame -> checks consensus -> summarises actions.

**B2 -> C1 progression:** B2 participates in priority negotiation. C1 manages the **interaction itself** and improves the discussion structure.

**Listening Preview:** usually unnecessary.

## Home & Services — 3 missions

### C1-HS-01 — Escalate a persistent service issue professionally ★ Core

**Goal:** explain a long-running service problem succinctly, show what has already been tried, request escalation, and keep the conversation focused on resolution rather than frustration.

**Communication problem:** `history + escalation + precise request + tone control`

**Likely shape:** summarise history -> provider repeats basic step/position -> learner explains why insufficient -> request appropriate escalation -> provider questions necessity -> learner justifies calmly -> agree owner/timeline/next action.

**B2 -> C1 progression:** B2 negotiates responsibility after service failure. C1 compresses a complex history and escalates strategically without becoming adversarial.

**Listening Preview:** optional/recommended.

---

### C1-HS-02 — Negotiate responsibility when the cause is uncertain

**Goal:** discuss a household/service problem whose cause could reasonably belong to more than one party, separate immediate repair from responsibility, and agree a fair investigation/next step.

**Communication problem:** `uncertain causation + responsibility + pragmatic sequencing`

**Likely shape:** describe issue/evidence -> provider offers interpretation -> learner questions certainty -> distinguish urgent action from final responsibility -> agree inspection/temporary solution -> clarify what happens after findings.

**C1 boundary:** authored facts control the uncertainty; no real insurance/legal advice.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

---

### C1-HS-03 — Explain complex requirements and repair a provider's assumptions

**Goal:** communicate a detailed but ordinary service requirement, notice when the provider simplifies it incorrectly, and reformulate until both sides share the same understanding.

**Communication problem:** `complex requirement + reformulation + precision`

**Likely shape:** explain requirements/priorities -> provider paraphrases inaccurately -> learner corrects distinction -> provider asks consequence -> learner gives example/priority -> jointly restate final scope -> confirm.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

## Plans & Leisure — 3 missions

### C1-PL-01 — Navigate a sensitive group disagreement about plans ★ Core

**Goal:** help a group make a plan when the practical disagreement also carries interpersonal sensitivity, such as someone feeling excluded, pressured, or ignored.

**Communication problem:** `group decision + implicit concern + tactful facilitation`

**Likely shape:** disagreement appears practical -> learner notices interpersonal concern -> asks/acknowledges it carefully -> restates priorities -> proposes inclusive options -> handles resistance -> summarises plan/common ground.

**B2 -> C1 progression:** B2 balances explicit preferences. C1 also manages the social meaning attached to those preferences.

**Listening Preview:** optional/recommended.

---

### C1-PL-02 — Coordinate an event when priorities are partly implicit

**Goal:** organise an event/outing when people state preferences indirectly or avoid saying what matters most, and bring enough clarity to make a decision without over-interpreting.

**Communication problem:** `implicit priorities + clarification + planning`

**Likely shape:** collect suggestions -> detect mismatch/hesitation -> ask open questions -> clarify non-negotiable vs preference -> propose option -> test reactions -> refine -> confirm.

**C1 boundary:** authored cues must support reasonable inference; no mind-reading.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

---

### C1-PL-03 — Handle a last-minute cancellation with relationship sensitivity

**Goal:** cancel/change an important social plan at short notice, explain enough context, acknowledge the impact on the other person, and preserve the relationship while proposing repair.

**Communication problem:** `bad news + explanation + empathy + repair`

**Likely shape:** deliver bad news appropriately -> other person reacts -> learner acknowledges inconvenience/disappointment -> gives proportionate explanation -> avoids over-defending -> proposes repair/alternative -> close.

**B2 -> C1 progression:** B2 changes plans under constraints. C1 manages the interpersonal consequences of the change.

**Listening Preview:** optional.

## What C1 deliberately leaves for C2

C1 can handle complex nuance effectively, but C2 should go further in:

- highly subtle implication and under-specified positions
- rapid reformulation with almost no loss of nuance
- layered mediation across several conflicting goals
- strategic switching of stance/register within the same interaction
- managing irony, allusion, or deliberately indirect language when materially relevant
- preserving distinctions between several very similar interpretations
- handling interactions where the best communicative move changes as hidden assumptions are uncovered

C1 is **nuanced and diplomatic**. C2 should feel **exceptionally flexible and precise**.

# Cross-level progression checks from B2

```text
B2 negotiate clear competing positions
-> C1 negotiate while facts, motives, or responsibility are partly ambiguous

B2 disagree politely
-> C1 criticise/challenge where face, power, or relationship risk changes the wording strategy

B2 challenge an inadequate service solution
-> C1 escalate a complex history proportionately and diplomatically

B2 balance explicit group preferences
-> C1 uncover and manage partly implicit social concerns

B2 participate effectively in a meeting disagreement
-> C1 structure/facilitate the interaction itself

B2 explain special requirements
-> C1 adapt the explanation to audience/register and repair subtle misinterpretation
```

If C1 difficulty can be reduced to “more constraints” with no real pragmatic/register/ambiguity challenge, the mission is probably still B2.
