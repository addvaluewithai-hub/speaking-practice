# C1 Practice Map

Status: **first full map reviewed and Core rebalanced**

This is a library map, not a required lesson sequence. `Core` means **Great place to start**, not prerequisite.

## Design boundary

C1 foregrounds **nuance, precision, register, ambiguity, tact, and relationship-sensitive choices**.

Typical shape:

- complex but plausible professional/social goal
- incomplete information or more than one reasonable interpretation
- learner chooses not only **what** to say, but **how** to say it
- explicit and implicit concerns may coexist
- tactful challenge, reframing, audience adaptation, or facilitation matters
- longer turns are natural, but length alone is not the difficulty
- several discourse paths may be valid

C1 should remain recognisably human and useful, not an abstract intellectual puzzle.

## Shared-English source anchors

Mission-specific grounding is selected only when a mission is promoted. Map design is informed by reviewed C1 abilities such as:

- adjusting formality/register/style to the social context
- tactfully steering an interaction toward a conclusion
- managing ambiguity and anticipating misunderstandings
- arguing a complex case precisely
- relating flexibly and effectively to the addressee
- dealing constructively with ambiguity in cross-cultural communication

These are design anchors, not learner item checklists.

## Core / Great place to start

The first draft over-selected complaints/conflict. The reviewed C1 Core deliberately demonstrates **different kinds of advanced communication**:

1. `C1-EV-03` — Explain the same complex decision to two different audiences — `register / audience adaptation`
2. `C1-PS-02` — Respond to an indirect concern and uncover what the person really means — `implication / tactful clarification`
3. `C1-FS-01` — Handle a serious service complaint with tact — `diplomatic escalation`
4. `C1-TT-02` — Explain an unusual travel requirement to different staff — `precision / role adaptation`
5. `C1-WS-03` — Facilitate a meeting with conflicting priorities and unclear goals — `facilitation / synthesis`
6. `C1-HS-03` — Explain complex requirements and repair a provider's assumptions — `reformulation / precision`
7. `C1-PL-02` — Coordinate an event when priorities are partly implicit — `implicit priorities / collaborative planning`

This mix is intentional: one conflict-heavy mission is enough for the entry set; the wider library still contains feedback, escalation, disagreement, and mediation practice.

Listening Preview is selective at C1. Use it only when a pragmatic/register exemplar adds real value.

# Mission library

## Everyday Life — 3

### C1-EV-01 — Handle ambiguous responsibility after a practical failure
**Goal:** resolve a practical failure when responsibility is uncertain without turning assumptions into accusations.
**Problem:** `ambiguity + responsibility + tactful resolution`
**Progression:** B2 negotiates clear positions; C1 negotiates while facts/responsibility remain partly uncertain.

### C1-EV-02 — De-escalate a tense practical conversation
**Goal:** acknowledge frustration, correct inaccurate framing, and steer a tense everyday disagreement toward a workable conclusion.
**Problem:** `emotion + reframing + de-escalation`

### C1-EV-03 — Explain the same complex decision to two different audiences ★ Core
**Goal:** explain one nuanced decision informally and then formally, changing detail/register without changing the core meaning.
**Problem:** `audience adaptation + register shift + precision`
**Progression:** difficulty comes from adapting expression to interlocutor, not from harder topic knowledge.

## People & Social — 3

### C1-PS-01 — Give sensitive feedback without damaging the relationship
**Goal:** describe a recurring behaviour/impact fairly, handle defensiveness, and agree a change while protecting the relationship.
**Problem:** `sensitive feedback + face management`

### C1-PS-02 — Respond to an indirect concern and uncover what the person really means ★ Core
**Goal:** notice an indirectly expressed concern, ask non-intrusive questions, check interpretation, and respond to the underlying issue.
**Problem:** `implication + tactful clarification + supportive response`
**Guardrail:** authored cues must support the inference; this is not mind-reading.

### C1-PS-03 — Mediate a relationship-sensitive misunderstanding
**Goal:** help two people distinguish intention from impact, reframe each perspective fairly, and find a way forward.
**Problem:** `mediation + perspective reframing + tact`

## Food & Shopping — 3

### C1-FS-01 — Handle a serious service complaint with tact ★ Core
**Goal:** explain a significant failure, prioritise the main issue, challenge a weak response precisely, and seek a proportionate solution without losing professionalism.
**Problem:** `complex complaint + prioritisation + diplomatic escalation`
**Progression:** B2 negotiates explicit trade-offs; C1 adds proportionality, tone, and escalation strategy.

### C1-FS-02 — Negotiate an exception when policy wording is ambiguous
**Goal:** clarify an ambiguously worded fictional policy and argue precisely for a fair interpretation/exception.
**Problem:** `ambiguous policy + precise argument + pragmatic negotiation`
**Guardrail:** no real legal-rights claims; scenario truth defines the ambiguity.

### C1-FS-03 — Resolve an expectation mismatch without accusing the other side
**Goal:** acknowledge literal compliance while explaining why the contextual expectation was different and negotiate a fair outcome.
**Problem:** `literal meaning vs reasonable expectation + nuanced framing`

## Travel & Transport — 3

### C1-TT-01 — Resolve a complex accommodation problem diplomatically
**Goal:** manage several linked accommodation issues, distinguish essential from flexible needs, and escalate proportionately.
**Problem:** `multi-issue complaint + prioritisation + tact`

### C1-TT-02 — Explain an unusual travel requirement to different staff ★ Core
**Goal:** communicate a non-routine but non-medical requirement across different staff roles, adapting detail/register and repairing mistaken assumptions.
**Problem:** `complex requirement + audience adaptation + repair`
**Progression:** B2 explains special requirements; C1 reshapes the explanation for different interlocutors.

### C1-TT-03 — Resolve ambiguous responsibility between two travel providers
**Goal:** stop circular blame between two providers, separate immediate need from later responsibility, and secure an actionable next step.
**Problem:** `multi-party ambiguity + precise clarification + escalation`

## Work & Study — 3

### C1-WS-01 — Give difficult professional feedback
**Goal:** deliver specific critical feedback respectfully, invite response, handle defence, and agree improvement.
**Problem:** `critical feedback + tact + collaborative improvement`

### C1-WS-02 — Challenge a manager/teacher decision tactfully
**Goal:** question a higher-authority decision, acknowledge its objective, explain consequences, and propose an alternative without sounding dismissive.
**Problem:** `power difference + tactful challenge + alternative`

### C1-WS-03 — Facilitate a meeting with conflicting priorities and unclear goals ★ Core
**Goal:** clarify what decision is actually needed, summarise positions, surface missing criteria, reframe the disagreement, and steer the meeting to usable actions.
**Problem:** `facilitation + ambiguity + synthesis`
**Progression:** B2 participates in priority negotiation; C1 improves and manages the interaction itself.

## Home & Services — 3

### C1-HS-01 — Escalate a persistent service issue professionally
**Goal:** compress a long service history, explain why basic steps are no longer enough, justify escalation, and agree ownership/timeline.
**Problem:** `history + escalation + precise request + tone control`

### C1-HS-02 — Negotiate responsibility when the cause is uncertain
**Goal:** separate immediate repair from final responsibility when the cause could belong to more than one party, then agree an investigation/temporary next step.
**Problem:** `uncertain causation + responsibility + pragmatic sequencing`

### C1-HS-03 — Explain complex requirements and repair a provider's assumptions ★ Core
**Goal:** communicate detailed ordinary requirements, detect when the provider has simplified them incorrectly, and reformulate until both sides share the same scope.
**Problem:** `complex requirement + reformulation + precision`
**Progression:** advanced difficulty comes from preserving distinctions while repairing paraphrase, not from specialist knowledge.

## Plans & Leisure — 3

### C1-PL-01 — Navigate a sensitive group disagreement about plans
**Goal:** manage a group decision where a practical disagreement also carries a concern about inclusion, pressure, or being ignored.
**Problem:** `group decision + implicit concern + tactful facilitation`

### C1-PL-02 — Coordinate an event when priorities are partly implicit ★ Core
**Goal:** bring enough clarity to indirect/unstated priorities to make an event decision without over-interpreting anyone.
**Problem:** `implicit priorities + clarification + collaborative planning`
**Guardrail:** the authored truth must provide fair cues; no mind-reading.

### C1-PL-03 — Handle a last-minute cancellation with relationship sensitivity
**Goal:** deliver bad news, acknowledge the impact, explain proportionately, avoid over-defending, and propose a credible repair/alternative.
**Problem:** `bad news + empathy + relationship repair`

## C1 -> C2 boundary

C1 is **nuanced and diplomatic**. C2 should go further in:

- preserving several subtle interpretations simultaneously
- rapid reformulation with almost no loss of nuance
- layered mediation across competing frameworks
- fast stance/register shifts within one interaction
- irony/allusion/strategic indirectness where genuinely contextual
- redesigning the interaction frame as hidden assumptions emerge

If C1 difficulty reduces to “more constraints” with no pragmatic/register/ambiguity challenge, it is probably still B2.
