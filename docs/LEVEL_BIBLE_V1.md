# Practice Level Bible v1

This document controls **mission interaction difficulty**, not the learner's entire English ability.

The mission author must stay inside the learner's overall CEFR boundary while also controlling interaction complexity, predictability, branching, turn length, and support.

## A1

Practice should feel safe, concrete, and highly predictable.

Typical mission shape:

- one clear real-world goal
- short turns
- 3–6 meaningful learner moves
- collaborative interlocutor
- familiar information
- little or no hidden complication
- tight conversation path
- strong hint ladder available
- learner may rely on short formulaic chunks

Good A1 mission goals:

- introduce yourself
- order one item
- ask a simple price
- give a basic personal detail
- ask where a place is
- check into a hotel with straightforward booking details

Avoid requiring extended explanation, negotiation, storytelling, or subtle register control.

## A2

Practice remains routine but allows more detail and small variation.

Typical mission shape:

- one main goal plus one secondary detail
- slightly longer turns
- simple preferences and reasons
- short follow-up chains
- one bounded variation or minor complication
- moderate predictability
- hints available but less necessary

Examples:

- order with preferences
- change a simple booking detail
- compare two familiar options
- explain a basic routine or need
- make a simple arrangement

## B1

Practice should require reasonably independent familiar communication.

Typical mission shape:

- clear goal with a meaningful complication
- connected answers rather than isolated phrases
- learner-owned follow-ups
- explanation or narration in short stretches
- multiple plausible branches
- partner remains realistic, not artificially difficult
- hints are optional support rather than expected scaffolding

Examples:

- resolve a wrong order
- explain a travel problem
- change a booking and accept/reject an alternative
- discuss plans and negotiate a workable option
- handle a familiar workplace misunderstanding

## B2

Practice should feel spontaneous and strategically demanding while staying in familiar or semi-familiar domains.

Typical mission shape:

- competing constraints or trade-offs
- alternatives and negotiation
- polite disagreement
- greater learner initiative
- longer natural turns
- less predictable AI wording
- richer branch graph
- need to maintain topic and repair interaction naturally

Examples:

- negotiate a service solution
- discuss project priorities
- challenge an unsuitable proposal politely
- compare alternatives under time/cost constraints

## C1

Practice should foreground nuance, precision, register, and extended reasoning.

Typical mission shape:

- complex professional or social goal
- nuanced stance
- tact and pragmatic choices
- ambiguity or incomplete information
- extended explanation and reformulation
- flexible topic management
- sophisticated but realistic branching

Examples:

- handle a sensitive workplace conversation
- negotiate expectations with tact
- explain a complex problem to different audiences
- disagree while preserving relationship and tone

## C2

Practice should test highly flexible communication rather than merely longer vocabulary.

Typical mission shape:

- subtle implication and ambiguity
- complex negotiation or mediation
- precise reformulation
- rapid adaptation to stance/register changes
- layered goals and interpersonal constraints
- high freedom inside a tightly authored truth model

C2 missions must not become artificial intellectual puzzles. They should remain plausible human interactions where linguistic flexibility genuinely matters.

## Cross-level authoring rule

Do not take one mission and mechanically add vocabulary to make it harder.

A higher-level mission should change the **communication problem** itself:

```text
A1: make a request
A2: make a request with preferences
B1: explain a problem with the request
B2: negotiate an alternative
C1: manage the problem with tact and nuanced constraints
C2: handle layered implications, competing positions, and precise reformulation
```

## Runtime variables controlled by level

Mission authors may tune:

- AI turn length
- speech density
- predictability
- number of required beats
- number/depth of branches
- information load
- learner initiative
- complication intensity
- degree of interpersonal nuance
- amount of support normally expected

A hint setting never changes the authored level.
